E-waste, batteries and recycling news

The stories that move India's recovery markets: EPR rules, e-waste, black mass, battery and rare earth recycling. India first, then the world. Summaries are ours; every story links to its source.

Rare earthsWorld19 Nov 2026

China's Pr-Nd oxide hits a monthly high as magnet-scrap prices climb with it

SMM's weekly review puts Pr-Nd oxide at 725,000–727,000 yuan/mt after an early-week dip to 715,000–718,000, while dysprosium oxide sits at 1.56–1.57 million yuan/mt and terbium oxide at 6.45–6.5 million. NdFeB scrap was quoted at 720–730 yuan/kg for Pr-Nd content, 1,230–1,250 yuan/kg for Dy and 5,600–5,800 yuan/kg for Tb.

Why it matters: Scrap payables track oxide prices closely, so firmer Pr-Nd and Dy lift the value of magnets recovered from Indian e-waste and motors.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

E-wasteWorld19 Nov 2026

Smelters pour money into e-scrap capacity, but the fight for feedstock has yet to heat up

Aurubis's Georgia smelter is built for about 180,000 tonnes a year, Atlantic Copper's new CirCular plant for about 60,000 tonnes, and Umicore has committed €450 million to expand Hoboken refining by 2030. Yet Boliden's Rönnskär took in 18,257 tonnes of electronics and alloy feed in Q3, down 1.8% year on year, and the 2026 copper concentrate benchmark treatment charge fell to zero.

Why it matters: With copper concentrate scarce and global smelters adding e-scrap lines, demand for Indian PCB and copper-bearing scrap could firm over the next few years.

Source: Resource Recycling

Battery recyclingIndia18 Nov 2026

India's battery recyclers face a purity test as black mass must meet battery-grade specs

Indian black mass typically holds about 35% nickel and cobalt by weight against a Chinese benchmark of over 40%, PwC India says, while the critical mineral recycling scheme sets indicative purity benchmarks of at least 99% for lithium, nickel and cobalt salts. Producers of EV and portable batteries must use recycled material equal to at least 5% of a battery's dry weight in FY28, rising to 20% from FY31.

Why it matters: With black mass exports now prohibited, recyclers' returns depend on refining it to grades that cell makers and the incentive scheme will accept.

Source: Outlook Business

PolicyWorld18 Nov 2026

China's commerce ministry and eight other departments issue guidelines to build out recycling networks, including EV batteries

Released on 17 November, the guidelines cover collection and dismantling of retired traction batteries, solar modules and wind turbine blades, and call for joint scrapping of electric vehicles with their batteries and a study of digital IDs for traction batteries. China aims to have a recycling system with full collection networks by 2030, with makers encouraged to run collection systems matched to their sales.

Why it matters: Tighter collection rules in China shape global black mass and battery scrap flows, and offer a template as India builds its own battery EPR system.

Source: SMM

Critical mineralsIndia16 Nov 2026

Technology Development Board backs Hyderabad's NeoSeekermetals to recover critical minerals from e-waste and battery scrap

The DST's Technology Development Board signed an agreement to fund a ₹20 crore project, with ₹10 crore approved from the government's RDI Fund as optionally convertible debt. NeoSeekermetals will use it to take its patented process for extracting critical minerals from e-waste and lithium-ion battery waste from TRL-5 to TRL-9.

Why it matters: Government money going into an indigenous recovery process matters for Indian recyclers now that black mass must be processed at home.

Source: Social News XYZ via IANS

EPRIndia13 Nov 2026

CPCB orders state inspections of over 2,000 recyclers, 309 of them e-waste units, to catch fake EPR claims

A July order from the CPCB chairman, reported by Reuters, asked all states to inspect more than 2,000 recyclers, including 309 e-waste processors, checking machinery, plant trial runs and electricity use against claimed output. Units found with forged or manipulated paperwork can be blocked from generating EPR certificates, and Uttar Pradesh, home to about a third of e-waste recyclers, has lined up 33 of them for immediate audits.

Why it matters: Producers buying EPR certificates face a higher risk that credits from weak recyclers get blocked, which should favour verified, high-capacity recyclers.

Source: Reuters via Business Standard

DealsIndia12 Nov 2026

COSMO AM&T and Carbon Resources plan a ₹40 billion LFP cathode plant in Andhra Pradesh

Andhra Pradesh's cabinet on 10 November approved a ₹40 billion plan by South Korea's COSMO AM&T and India's Carbon Resources for a battery materials plant at Naidupeta in Tirupati district. The first phase, targeted for mid-2028, would make 30,000 tonnes a year each of iron phosphate precursor and LFP cathode material, rising to 180,000 tonnes each (360,000 tonnes combined) by 2031.

Why it matters: A large domestic LFP cathode maker would be a natural local buyer for lithium recovered by Indian recyclers, which matters more now that black mass can no longer be exported.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Black massIndia11 Nov 2026

India bans exports of lithium-ion battery black mass with immediate effect

A DGFT notification moves battery waste and scrap under ITC (HS) code 85491400 from 'Free' to 'Prohibited' until further orders, covering unsorted scrap free of lead, cadmium and mercury, which the notice identifies as lithium-ion black mass. The change is limited to that category and is not a blanket ban on all battery material.

Why it matters: Indian shredders that sold black mass abroad must now find domestic refiners, which should shift payables and favour recyclers with their own hydrometallurgy capacity.

Source: ANI

Solar wasteIndia5 Nov 2026

MNRE is drafting solar module recycling guidelines that will push makers to build more recyclable panels

MNRE Secretary Santosh Kumar Sarangi said at the Bharat Renewable Energy Summit that the ministry wants the guidelines out quickly; solar and wind waste currently falls under the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, which require waste modules to be preserved until 2035. Suzlon and the Aditya Birla Group signed an MoU to recycle blade epoxy, and MNRE plans an SOP to extend this to other wind turbine makers.

Why it matters: Separate product-specific rules for solar and wind waste would reshape EPR obligations for module makers and open a dedicated feedstock stream for Indian recyclers ahead of the 2033–35 wave of retiring panels.

Source: Business Standard

Critical mineralsIndia4 Nov 2026

Cabinet clears ₹2,814 crore capex subsidy for two lithium and two nickel processing plants

The five-year scheme (FY27 to FY31) offers a 30% capital subsidy to four units, each lithium plant needing at least 30,000 tonnes of capacity and each nickel plant at least 50,000 tonnes, with cobalt recovered as a by-product of nickel. Support is capped at ₹500 crore per lithium beneficiary and ₹900 crore per nickel beneficiary, picked through technical qualification followed by bidding on the lowest capital support sought.

Why it matters: It funds the step of turning ores and intermediates into high-purity battery compounds, which India largely lacks today; whether recycled intermediates such as black mass qualify will decide how much it helps Indian battery recyclers.

Source: Business Standard

EPRIndia3 Nov 2026

Lead industry body wants India's battery dealers made accountable for collecting spent batteries

India Lead Zinc Development Association executive director L Pugazhenthy says the Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 leave dealers' duties unclear, and wants dealers registered, collections reported and recyclers audited regularly rather than approved once. About 30% of India's recycled lead still comes from the informal sector and about 65% from authorised recyclers, even though authorised capacity of about 3.5 million tonnes is well above secondary lead output of about 1.6 million tonnes.

Why it matters: Tighter dealer and audit rules would steer more spent lead-acid batteries to authorised recyclers and change how producers meet their battery EPR targets.

Source: Batteries International

E-wasteIndia31 Oct 2026

Wayanad gathers 80 tonnes of e-waste in a 50-day drive to become India's first e-waste-free district

The Kerala campaign, launched on 10 September, covered all 23 village panchayats and three municipalities, with government offices handing in 31 tonnes and Noolpuzha becoming the first e-waste-free panchayat on 27 October after collecting 5,423 kg. A buffer period with a complaints call centre runs to 10 November, before a formal declaration at Kalpetta on 20 November.

Why it matters: If other districts copy this government-led model, more household and office e-waste could move into formal collection, the feedstock India's EPR-registered recyclers compete for.

Source: Onmanorama

PolicyIndia31 Oct 2026

GST reverse charge now applies to e-waste scrap bought from unregistered sellers

From 1 November 2026, a registered business that buys electrical and electronic waste and scrap (tariff item 8549) from an unregistered supplier must pay the GST itself under reverse charge, under Notification No. 04/2026 dated 27 October 2026. The same change covers plastic scrap, tyre scrap and used cooking oil, and was made on the GST Council's recommendation.

Why it matters: Recyclers and dismantlers that source e-waste from informal kabadiwalas now carry the GST liability and paperwork on those purchases, which changes sourcing costs and pushes the chain toward registered suppliers.

Source: SCC Times (SCC Online)

Rare earthsIndia29 Oct 2026

Odisha plans a rare-earth corridor around Gopalpur, from beach-sand mining to magnet making

Odisha is drawing up an integrated rare-earth cluster on the Ganjam-Puri coast covering extraction, separation, metals and permanent magnets, building on its roughly 3.16 million tonnes of monazite (of 13.15 million tonnes nationally). The state has approved five projects with proposed investment of about ₹26,450 crore and around 18,450 jobs, while the Mining Corporation of Odisha is acquiring new beach-sand blocks.

Why it matters: Rare earth chloride from IREL's Odisha plant is now processed elsewhere, so a local magnet supply chain would create domestic offtake, and future scrap streams, for India's planned magnet recyclers.

Source: Business Standard

Battery recyclingWorld17 Nov 2026

Two UK battery recycling projects using University of Leicester technology win £5.4 million

The £3.7 million STOCKMAN project will develop a hydrometallurgical route to recover lithium, nickel, cobalt and graphite, while the £1.7 million RECLAIM project, led by Ecoshred and Lithium Battery Recyclers, will trial direct recycling. Both are backed by the Battery Innovation Programme delivered by Innovate UK.

Why it matters: Direct-recycling and graphite-recovery methods being funded abroad are options Indian recyclers can watch as they look to move beyond black mass.

Source: East Midlands Business Link

PolicyWorld17 Nov 2026

North Carolina bans lithium-ion batteries from landfills and most incinerators from 1 December

North Carolina's ban on sending lithium-ion batteries to landfills and most permitted incinerators starts on 1 December, pushing local programmes to take loose batteries and battery-containing devices at drop-off points. The state, which holds more than 80% of US lithium ore reserves in two counties, is also asking residents to keep batteries and electronics out of kerbside recycling bins.

Why it matters: Disposal bans are a proven way to raise collection volumes, a lesson India's battery EPR system can use as it tries to pull spent cells away from the informal sector.

Source: Resource Recycling

Solar wasteWorld16 Nov 2026

US recycler SPR takes a fifth plant in California, lifting its capacity towards 6.5 million panels a year

Solar Panel Recycling has taken over a 42,000-square-foot site in Porterville, California, which will start at 1 million panels a year once retrofitted, with processing hoped for by February. With four other sites in North Carolina, Texas and Georgia, the company says its total capacity will reach 6.5 million panels a year and that it recovers at least 95% of the aluminium, glass and silver.

Why it matters: It shows the plant scale and recovery rates solar recyclers are reaching abroad, a benchmark for India as MNRE drafts module recycling guidelines.

Source: Resource Recycling

Rare earthsWorld15 Nov 2026

Ex-China Pr-Nd oxide rises $4/kg in a week as dysprosium and terbium firm in Rotterdam

SMM's weekly ex-China assessment puts Pr-Nd oxide at $113–125/kg FOB, up $4/kg, with Pr-Nd alloy also up $4/kg at $130–144/kg FOB. In Rotterdam bonded warehouses, dysprosium oxide gained $50/kg to $3,400–4,200/kg and terbium oxide $100/kg to $7,500–8,300/kg.

Why it matters: Firmer prices outside China lift the value of the neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium and terbium locked in scrap magnets, improving the case for Indian magnet-recycling projects.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld13 Nov 2026

US magnet recycler REEcycle clears an SEC hurdle for its planned Nasdaq listing via a SPAC

The SEC has declared effective the registration statement for REEcycle's merger with Hall Chadwick Acquisition Corp., a deal that values the Oklahoma rare-earth magnet recycler at about US$350 million plus a US$50 million earnout. Shareholders vote on 10 December, and REEcycle's first commercial plant is planned at roughly 160 tonnes a year of processing capacity.

Why it matters: A public listing for a magnet-to-oxide recycler gives Indian investors and recyclers a live valuation benchmark for rare-earth recovery from end-of-life magnets.

Source: Hall Chadwick Acquisition Corp. via GlobeNewswire

Battery recyclingWorld13 Nov 2026

South Korea plans regulatory exemptions to recycle more cathode and anode scrap from battery plants

At a 12 November meeting in Daejeon, South Korea's environment ministry said it will designate circular-economy zones with regulatory exemptions by the end of 2026 and back recycling trials through its regulatory sandbox in the first quarter of 2027. Companies asked for wider circular-resource eligibility so that production scrap now classed as waste faces lighter storage and transport rules.

Why it matters: With black mass exports now banned, Indian recyclers and cell makers will be watching how Korea frees up gigafactory scrap from waste rules, a model India's own battery-waste regime may need.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Black massWorld13 Nov 2026

Veolia opens a French hydrometallurgical plant able to process nearly 7,000 tonnes of black mass a year

Veolia said on 12 November it has started a battery-metals recycling facility in France's Grand Est region that can treat nearly 7,000 tonnes of black mass a year, roughly 20,000 tonnes of batteries, recovering over 90% of the cobalt, nickel and copper. Feedstock will come from France, the UK and Switzerland, and Veolia expects close to 700,000 tonnes of batteries to reach recycling in Europe within five years.

Why it matters: More refining capacity in Europe adds buyers for black mass just as India has banned its export, so Indian recyclers will be competing with hubs like this for feedstock and offtake partners rather than selling to them.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Solar wasteWorld12 Nov 2026

Australia cancels the tender for its $24.7 million national solar panel recycling pilot

Australia's environment department scrapped the procurement for the pilot without awarding a contract, after months of suspension over a complaint; the scheme was meant to collect up to 250,000 panels from up to 100 sites over 12 to 18 months. The minister has asked for urgent advice on next steps and says the government remains committed to a national pilot.

Why it matters: It shows how slowly solar-waste take-back schemes can get off the ground, a useful caution as India's MNRE drafts its own module recycling guidelines.

Source: RenewEconomy

Rare earthsWorld12 Nov 2026

Korean firms commit 650 billion won to rare-earth magnet plants ahead of US defence sourcing curbs

LS Cable & System will spend 350 billion won ($263 million) on a magnet plant in Gumi with more than 1,000 tonnes a year of capacity from 2028, while KMMI plans 300 billion won through 2030 for a 6,000-tonne plant in Pohang. The push comes before US rules from 1 January 2027 restrict China-sourced magnets across the whole defence supply chain.

Why it matters: Non-Chinese magnet capacity is growing fast in Asia, which raises demand for traceable rare-earth feed, including recycled material that Indian magnet and e-waste recyclers could supply.

Source: The Korea Herald

Rare earthsWorld12 Nov 2026

China's magnet-scrap prices hold at highs as dysprosium oxide climbs past 1.5 million yuan a tonne

SMM's weekly review puts Pr-Nd recovered from NdFeB scrap at 715–720 yuan/kg, recycled dysprosium at 1,230–1,250 yuan/kg and recycled terbium at 5,600–5,800 yuan/kg after successive gains. Dysprosium oxide rose sharply to 1.51–1.52 million yuan/mt and Pr-Nd oxide to 715,000–718,000 yuan/mt as of 12 November.

Why it matters: Firm heavy rare earth prices improve the economics of recovering magnets from Indian e-waste and motors, a segment the critical-mineral recycling scheme is meant to build up.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Battery recyclingWorld11 Nov 2026

Germany's Tozero makes first delivery of recycled lithium carbonate under a long-term contract

Tozero shipped lithium carbonate from its Gendorf demonstration plant, sized for about 500 tonnes of battery waste a year, to ceramic-colour maker Botz under a binding supply deal. The startup aims to recover over 80% of the lithium in used batteries and sells mainly to construction, ceramics and lubricant buyers for now.

Why it matters: It shows small black-mass refiners can earn early revenue from non-battery lithium buyers while working towards battery grade, a path open to Indian start-ups too.

Source: electrive

Black massWorld10 Nov 2026

LS MnM opens an Ulsan battery materials plant that can take black mass as well as MHP

The South Korean smelter completed the plant at its Onsan site on 10 November, with nickel sulfate capacity equal to 24,000 tonnes of contained nickel a year. It will also make cobalt sulfate, manganese sulfate and lithium hydroxide for precursor makers.

Why it matters: More Korean refining capacity that accepts black mass adds to the buyers Asian recyclers can sell to, a market Indian exporters are now shut out of.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

Solar wasteWorld10 Nov 2026

Ecoprogetti's automated solar panel recycling line is running in northern Italy

Ecoprogetti has supplied a fully automated module recycling line to a site in Sale, Italy, run by Raee.man, a subsidiary of Haiki+. The line can handle up to 120 panels an hour and has reported 94% uptime since it began operating in early 2025.

Why it matters: Indian module makers and recyclers preparing for MNRE's coming solar recycling guidelines can use this as a benchmark for what a commercial automated line delivers.

Source: PV Tech

DealsWorld10 Nov 2026

Rivian widens Redwood Materials deal to cover scrap, end-of-life packs and second-life storage

Redwood Materials will recycle Rivian's production scrap and retired EV batteries collected through Rivian's service centres, deciding pack by pack whether to reuse or recycle. An earlier April 2026 deal is already deploying 10 MWh of storage built from repurposed Rivian packs at its Normal, Illinois plant, and Redwood says it recovers over 95% of lithium, nickel, cobalt and copper.

Why it matters: It shows the full-lifecycle OEM–recycler contract that Indian EV makers may need to meet battery EPR targets, combining reuse and recycling under one partner.

Source: Business Wire via Batteries News

Black massWorld9 Nov 2026

China's black mass payables fell through October, with LCO dropping hardest

SMM says the lithium payable for LCO black mass slid from above 70% to about 63% in October, while ternary black mass cobalt payables fell from 73.46% to 68.9% and lithium payables to 70.02%. The cuts followed a month in which lithium carbonate fell more than 20% and cobalt sulfate over 25%.

Why it matters: Lower Chinese payables set the benchmark Indian black mass sellers are quoted against, squeezing margins for recyclers that sell rather than refine.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

Rare earthsWorld9 Nov 2026

China extends its pause on wider rare earth export controls by two months, to 10 January

China's Ministry of Commerce said on 9 November that the one-year suspension of its October 2025 expansion of rare earth export controls, which also covered lithium battery technology, will run two more months until 10 January. The move is part of a wider pause in trade countermeasures as Beijing negotiates a lasting deal with Washington ahead of an expected Xi–Trump meeting at APEC in Shenzhen.

Why it matters: A short two-month window keeps rare earth and magnet supply uncertain, which supports the case for Indian magnet and battery recyclers building domestic sources of these materials.

Source: Reuters via Investing.com

PolicyWorld5 Nov 2026

US EPA says its draft universal-waste rule for lithium-ion batteries and solar panels may be out for comment in spring

EPA officials told state waste regulators that the draft rule, first promised in 2023, is still being written and could be proposed for public comment in the spring, with the federal agenda pencilling in a final rule for August 2027. State officials estimate only about 5% of US lithium batteries are recycled, and a House-passed bill would force EPA to finish a lithium-ion rule within 18 months.

Why it matters: Clearer US rules on collecting and storing spent lithium-ion batteries and end-of-life panels would change how much feedstock stays with US recyclers, which shapes export competition and partnership openings for Indian battery and solar recyclers.

Source: Waste Dive

Black massWorld5 Nov 2026

China LFP black mass prices edge down again while ternary payables hold steady

SMM said lithium carbonate consolidated around 92,000 yuan per tonne as of 5 November, while LFP black mass prices were adjusted down modestly and cobalt payables for ternary material slipped slightly. LCO black mass payables remain near their lowest levels of the past year, with trading described as sluggish and buyers waiting.

Why it matters: Chinese black mass payables set the benchmark against which Indian shredders price their output and judge whether exporting or refining locally pays.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

Battery recyclingWorld4 Nov 2026

Wagga residents press NSW planning commission to reject a 3,000-tonne lithium-ion battery recycling hub

The NSW Independent Planning Commission held public meetings on 3 and 4 November on Calibre Metals' proposed battery recycling hub in East Wagga, which the developer says could process at most 3,000 tonnes a year. The planning department received more than 50 community submissions opposing it, with flood risk a recurring concern, and written submissions stay open until 11 November.

Why it matters: Local opposition over fire and flood risk is a hurdle Indian battery recyclers also face when siting plants, and shows why site choice and safety cases matter for approvals.

Source: The Daily Advertiser

DealsWorld3 Nov 2026

Malaysian battery recycler Econili adds Vynn Capital, taking total funding to RM8 million

Econili, one of five licensed battery-recycling operators in Malaysia, has 24,000 tonnes a year of battery treatment and 12,000 tonnes of critical-mineral recovery capacity in Ipoh, recovering lithium, nickel and cobalt. The undisclosed Vynn Capital cheque brings total funding to about RM8 million (US$1.96 million), to be used to buy more feedstock and raise throughput.

Why it matters: Southeast Asian recyclers raising money to buy more battery scrap adds to regional competition for the feedstock Indian recyclers also import.

Source: Digital News Asia

Solar wasteWorld2 Nov 2026

Japan proposes that owners scrapping 50 tonnes or more of solar panels file disposal plans in advance

Japan's environment ministry and METI favour a 50-tonne threshold that would cover about 7,398 facilities and roughly 38% of expected panel waste from 2027 to 2050, under a solar recycling law due to take effect around December 2027. Panel waste is now projected to peak at up to about 500,000 tonnes a year in the 2040s, well above earlier estimates.

Why it matters: Japan's volume-based trigger is a working template for India as it decides how to make large solar plant owners handle their own coming wave of end-of-life modules.

Source: pv magazine via Solar Now

Black massWorld2 Nov 2026

Sumitomo moves past an MoU to study black mass processing at Livium's Melbourne site

Sumitomo Corporation and Livium's Envirostream unit are now evaluating technology, equipment, economics and approvals for expanding black mass output at Derrimut, Victoria, with possible refining in Japan. Sumitomo has started onboarding for a potential black mass purchase, but no binding deal, volumes or pricing are agreed, and Envirostream's existing SungEel HiTech offtake runs to June 2027.

Why it matters: Japanese trading houses lining up black mass from smaller markets is the same feedstock competition Indian recyclers face as they try to keep black mass at home.

Source: Mining.com.au

Rare earthsWorld2 Nov 2026

Fraunhofer and TU Freiberg test two new routes to recover neodymium from hard-drive magnets

The four-year, Horizon Europe-funded NEO-CYCLE project is trialling solid-state chlorination with ammonium chloride and an electrochemical process to separate neodymium, iron and boron from scrapped magnets, with a pilot plant already being planned. Fraunhofer notes about 90% of Europe's neodymium comes from China and most magnets collected in Europe are shipped there for reprocessing.

Why it matters: Hard drives are a large part of India's e-waste stream, so low-chemical magnet recovery routes are directly relevant to recyclers eyeing rare earths under the critical-mineral recycling scheme.

Source: Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft

Solar wasteWorld1 Nov 2026

Comstock Metals says its Nevada solar panel recycling line now runs continuously

Comstock Metals reports that its solar panel recycling system at Silver Springs, Nevada has moved to continuous operation across four shift teams and will now ramp up production. It also says extra storage for incoming panels is in place at its Hanford, California and Cambridge, Ohio sites.

Why it matters: Commercial-scale panel recycling running round the clock in the US is a reference point for India, which has yet to build dedicated capacity for its own coming wave of solar waste.

Source: American Recycler via Now.Solar

Battery recyclingWorld30 Oct 2026

China's GEM recycled 50,105 tonnes of EV batteries in January–September, up over 36%

GEM, one of China's largest battery recyclers, says it recycled and dismantled 50,105 tonnes of power batteries in the first three quarters of 2026. That is more than 36% higher than a year earlier, according to its third-quarter report.

Why it matters: Fast-growing Chinese recyclers set the scale and cost benchmark that Indian battery recyclers and black mass buyers will have to compete against.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Solar wasteWorld30 Oct 2026

Australia faces 1.1 million tonnes of dead solar panels by 2035 and needs national rules now, RAND–RMIT brief warns

A policy brief by RAND and RMIT University, sponsored by Australia's industry department, projects cumulative end-of-life solar waste of 1.1 million tonnes by 2035, possibly tripling by 2050, and urges a national PV circularity framework by mid-2027 and federal funding for recycling plants by 2028. It values recoverable silver at more than A$6 billion (about US$4.2 billion) a year by 2035 and finds a 2,500-tonne-a-year recycling plant could pay back in 7.2 to 8 years in five states.

Why it matters: India's solar fleet is growing far faster than Australia's, so the brief's plant economics and reuse-first approach are a useful benchmark for Indian recyclers planning panel capacity.

Source: pv magazine Australia via Now.Solar
