E-waste, batteries and recycling news

The stories that move India's recovery markets: EPR rules, e-waste, black mass, battery and rare earth recycling. India first, then the world. Summaries are ours; every story links to its source.

 

Latest

Rare earthsWorld27 Oct 2026

HyProMag USA plans three more US magnet recycling plants to quadruple output to about 4,200 tonnes a year

A pre-feasibility study puts new plants in South Carolina, Nevada and a second Texas site at about $623–656 million, lifting salable NdFeB magnet output to roughly 4,192 tonnes a year by 2034 from about 1,048 tonnes at its first plant in Northlake, Texas. The study, which modelled a recycled-scrap-only case among others, estimates a post-tax NPV of $1.85–2.06 billion; Northlake's first phase is due in mid-2028 and no offtake deals are signed yet.

Why it matters: Magnet scrap is becoming a contested feedstock outside China, which matters for Indian recyclers that collect NdFeB scrap from motors and electronics and for India's planned magnet plants.

Source: Investing.com

Rare earthsWorld24 Oct 2026

Taiwan's ITRI plans a 500-tonne-a-year rare earth plant to meet half the island's needs by 2030

ITRI president Chang Pei-zen says a demonstration plant making 500 tonnes of rare earths a year is targeted for completion by end-2028, a hundredfold scale-up from the institute's experimental line of about 5,000 kg a year. The goal is to cover half of Taiwan's demand by end-2030 and cut exposure to Chinese export restrictions, with state funding and possible price guarantees still under discussion.

Why it matters: It is another electronics-heavy economy building rare earth separation outside China, the same supply gap India's magnet and critical-mineral recycling schemes are trying to close.

Source: Focus Taiwan (CNA)

Solar wasteWorld24 Oct 2026

South Korea starts recycling solar panels on site with mobile trailer plants as panel waste climbs

Waste solar panels processed in South Korea rose from 152 tonnes in 2023 to 2,300 tonnes in 2026, and the first on-site recycling with trailer-mounted units ran this week under a regulatory sandbox that exempts it from the Wastes Control Act. A recycler says processing at the solar plant can cut transport costs to about one-sixth, while a blade-based method that separates glass from cells lifts silver and copper recovery four- to fivefold.

Why it matters: As India's early solar fleet ages, on-site processing and better silver recovery show Indian recyclers ways to cut the freight costs that make panel recycling hard to pay for.

Source: SBS News

Solar wasteWorld23 Oct 2026

Rystad puts the value of recyclable solar panel materials at €1.7 billion today, rising to €69 billion by 2050

Rystad Energy estimates recycled silver could cover 21% of solar manufacturers' needs in 2035, against only about 2% for copper. Separately, a University of Newcastle pilot processed material equivalent to 468 kg of rooftop panels and recovered close to 100% of the silver.

Why it matters: Silver is the main value driver in end-of-life panels, so Indian recyclers preparing for solar waste under the e-waste EPR regime need recovery routes that capture it.

Source: Recycling International via Now.Solar

India

EPRIndia22 Oct 2026

Bhupender Yadav says over 86,000 producers and 4,880 recyclers are now registered under India's EPR rules

Speaking at a CII Circular Economy Conference in New Delhi, the Environment Minister said more than 86,043 producers and 4,880 recyclers are registered under EPR frameworks covering plastic, battery, e-waste and tyre waste. He said nearly 499.60 lakh tonnes of waste have been processed across these regulated streams so far.

Why it matters: The minister tied EPR certificate transfers to recycler income and capacity growth, signalling the government sees the certificate market as the main lever for formal recycling.

Source: ThePrint (via PTI)

PolicyIndia20 Oct 2026

Mines Ministry panel to rewrite the 2019 National Mineral Policy, naming recycling and critical minerals as priorities

The Mines Ministry has formed a nine-member panel, chaired by an Additional Secretary, to review the National Mineral Policy, 2019 and recommend a comprehensive revision within two months, PTI reports citing sources. Recycling, critical minerals and deep-sea resources are named as the new priorities, alongside a planned recast of the MMDR Act, 1957.

Why it matters: If recycling gets a formal place in national mineral policy, it could shape future incentives and rules for India's e-waste and battery recyclers.

Source: Business Standard (via PTI)

E-wasteIndia15 Oct 2026

Wayanad aims to be India's first e-waste-free district by 1 November

Kerala's Wayanad district collected 31 tonnes of e-waste from government offices in the first phase of its drive (28–30 September), including 12 tonnes each from Kalpetta and Bathery and five from Mananthawady. The material goes to the state's Clean Kerala Company, and the district is targeting e-waste-free status by Keralam Formation Day on 1 November.

Why it matters: District drives that route institutional e-waste to a state agency show where formal recyclers can find steady volumes, and who they may be competing with for them.

Source: India Press Agency (IPA Newspack)

Solar wasteIndia15 Oct 2026

Chennai workshop calls for a separate rulebook for end-of-life solar panels

Speakers at a Consumer and Civic Action Group workshop in Chennai said India needs a dedicated solar recycling policy, modelled on its e-waste rules. With a 280 GW solar target, they argued EPR alone has not delivered safe panel dismantling.

Why it matters: Solar panels sit inside the e-waste rules today. A separate framework would change who pays, and what recyclers can claim.

Source: The Hindu (via Solar Now)

Battery recyclingIndia14 Oct 2026

N.A.N. Silox GreenMet breaks ground on ₹1,400 crore battery recycling plant in Andhra Pradesh

The 50:50 venture between India's N.A.N. GreenMet and Belgium's Silox Group has secured Consent to Establish and started work on a lithium-ion battery recycling and critical-metals plant at Naidupeta. Capacity will be added in phases up to 40,000 tonnes a year of shredding and 20,000 tonnes a year of hydrometallurgical processing, making battery-grade salts; Vijay Pasupathy has been named CEO.

Why it matters: A domestic hydromet buyer of this size adds competition for Indian black mass and battery scrap, and moves the country closer to refining battery salts at home instead of shipping out intermediates.

Source: Indian Chemical News

Rare earthsIndia14 Oct 2026

Nexon Geochem plans ₹1,250 crore rare-earth magnet plant in Andhra Pradesh

Hyderabad-based Nexon Geochem has signed an MoU with the Andhra Pradesh Economic Development Board for a sintered NdFeB magnet plant at Thondangi in Kakinada district, with ₹750 crore in phase one and ₹500 crore in phase two. The plant is designed to make 600 tonnes of permanent magnets a year for EVs, wind turbines, industrial automation and defence.

Why it matters: No dates were given, but a domestic sintered-magnet maker would give Indian rare-earth recyclers a potential local outlet for recovered NdPr and magnet scrap.

Source: Business Standard (via PTI)

E-wasteIndia14 Oct 2026

India reports 13.98 lakh tonnes of e-waste for 2024-25, but still doesn't count the informal workforce

Published on International E-Waste Day, this piece notes the environment ministry's estimate of 13.98 lakh tonnes generated in 2024-25, with 70.7% reported as processed. It argues India tracks tonnes and certificates but not the informal workers who collect and dismantle most of it.

Why it matters: Collection targets rise to 80% from FY2027-28. The informal channel is where the missing tonnes are.

Source: The Wire

World roundup

PolicyWorld22 Oct 2026

Thailand proposes banning metal scrap imports to curb e-waste smuggling

Thailand's Customs Department on 21 October proposed a ban on imports of ferrous, copper, aluminium and rubber scrap, with a 6–12 month transition period, to fight smuggling of electronic and hazardous waste. Customs has already stepped up physical checks on containers declared as metal scrap, though the ban itself is still only a proposal.

Why it matters: A Thai ban would push more scrap toward other Asian importers such as India and signals tighter regional policing of e-waste shipped as metal scrap.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

GM ends magnet supply deal with VAC's new $500 million South Carolina plant

General Motors terminated its strategic sourcing agreement with e-VAC Magnetics, the US unit of Germany's Vacuumschmelze, effective 1 July 2026; the plant has capacity of just under 2,000 tonnes a year and was meant to send 90% of its output to GM. VAC has begun arbitration seeking damages, and Energy Fuels, which agreed in June to buy VAC for $1.9 billion, says the price could be adjusted depending on the outcome.

Why it matters: Weaker carmaker demand for non-Chinese magnets shapes the market Indian magnet makers and rare-earth recyclers are planning to sell into.

Source: Reuters via StreetInsider

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

Neo Performance Materials wins up to €126 million in EU funding to expand its Estonian magnet plant

The EU Innovation Fund grant will help lift Neo's magnet capacity in Estonia from 2,000 to about 5,000 tonnes a year, with a third of the new output aimed at EU offshore wind. The grant agreement is due to be signed in March 2027, and construction is expected to take two to three years.

Why it matters: Europe is paying to build magnet capacity outside China, a benchmark for the incentives India offers its own rare-earth magnet and recycling projects.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Black massWorld22 Oct 2026

China black mass payables slide again as lithium carbonate falls about 3,000 yuan in a week

SMM's weekly recycling review says lithium carbonate started the week near 100,000 yuan/mt and was quoted about 3,000 yuan lower by 22 October, dragging recycled material prices down. Ternary black mass payables kept grinding lower, while LCO black mass payables fell faster on weak demand.

Why it matters: Chinese payables set the reference for Asian black mass trade, so falling coefficients squeeze margins for Indian recyclers selling or importing black mass.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

China's NdFeB scrap Pr-Nd prices keep sliding as oxide prices weaken

Shanghai Metals Market says praseodymium-neodymium recovered from NdFeB magnet scrap fell again this week to 735-745 yuan/kg, tracking lower Pr-Nd oxide prices, while scrap dysprosium closed at 1,230-1,250 yuan/kg and terbium at 5,600-5,800 yuan/kg. Recyclers cut their quotes and bought less, holders were reluctant to sell, and SMM expects prices to stay weak in the short term.

Why it matters: Chinese scrap magnet prices set the benchmark for what Indian e-waste and motor recyclers can expect for recovered magnets, so a falling market squeezes margins on magnet recovery projects.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld20 Oct 2026

China's rare earth magnet exports hit a 2026 high of about 6,165 tonnes in September

China exported 6,165 tonnes of rare earth permanent magnets in September, up 23% from August and 6.7% from a year earlier, the highest monthly figure this year, as backlogged orders were released, according to SMM. Exports for January to September totalled 48,055 tonnes, up 20% year on year.

Why it matters: SMM counts India among the markets driving demand, and steadier Chinese magnet supply sets the price bar that India's planned magnet plants and magnet recyclers must compete with.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld20 Oct 2026

Phoenix Tailings plans $375 million rare earth separation plant in New York that will also take recycled feed

Phoenix Tailings will build a plant in Rochester, New York, to process more than 18,000 tons a year of mined and recycled rare earth feedstock into over 4,400 tons of separated oxides, ramping up in 2027 and reaching full capacity in 2028. It is part of a $1.2 billion project backed by a $500 million conditional loan commitment from the US Office of Strategic Capital.

Why it matters: Large Western separation capacity that accepts recycled feed creates more buyers for magnet scrap outside China, a market Indian magnet recyclers could target.

Source: MINING.COM

Critical mineralsWorld20 Oct 2026

Öko-Institut study says recycling batteries in the EU could cut virgin cobalt demand by 60-70%

An Öko-Institut study commissioned by members of the EU Raw Materials Coalition finds that recycling lithium-ion batteries inside the EU could reduce virgin demand by 25-50% for lithium, 60-70% for cobalt and 35-50% for nickel. It also says smaller EVs and lighter batteries could trim mineral needs by about 17% by 2035, and calls for specific recycling targets and more funding for recycling plants.

Why it matters: It gives Indian policymakers and recyclers a benchmark for how much recycling can offset imported lithium, cobalt and nickel as India builds out its own critical-mineral recycling incentives.

Source: Resource Recycling

Battery recyclingWorld20 Oct 2026

Korea's recyclers struggle to make money from LFP batteries as their share of EVs grows

LFP cells held over 65% of the global EV cathode market in January–August 2026, but their recoverable value rests mostly on lithium (about $9.59/kg last year) rather than cobalt ($34.21/kg) or nickel ($15.2/kg). Korea recovered 65.9% of mandatory-return EV batteries between 2021 and August 2026, and industry is asking for recycled-content mandates and clarity on who pays to treat low-value LFP packs.

Why it matters: India's EV and storage fleet is leaning heavily on LFP, so Indian recyclers and EPR planners face the same question of how to fund recycling of chemistries with little cobalt or nickel to sell.

Source: MoneyToday (Korea)

Black massWorld19 Oct 2026

China's black mass output fell about 15% in September as LFP supply tightened

SMM estimates China produced roughly 56,000 tonnes of lithium battery black mass in September, about 15% less than in August, with LFP black mass driving most of the drop. Stricter rules on reusing old batteries, plant maintenance and sellers holding back scrap all cut volumes, while falling lithium carbonate and cobalt sulfate prices squeezed recycler margins.

Why it matters: Lower Chinese black mass output and thinner margins affect the payables and export demand Indian battery recyclers see for their own black mass.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Battery recyclingWorld19 Oct 2026

POSCO's Korean recycling plant turns 12,000 tonnes of black mass a year into battery salts

POSCO's battery recycling unit says its South Korean hydrometallurgy plant processes about 12,000 tonnes of black mass a year into 2,500 tonnes of lithium carbonate, 12,000 tonnes of nickel sulfate, 3,800 tonnes of cobalt sulfate and 2,000 tonnes of manganese sulfate. The plant reports recovering over 90% of the main valuable metals, and SNE Research expects used-battery volumes to grow from 44 GWh in 2025 to 3,339 GWh in 2040.

Why it matters: It is a benchmark for the scale and recovery rates Indian black mass refiners will need to compete with as they move beyond shredding.

Source: MoneyToday

Solar wasteWorld18 Oct 2026

Thailand plans dedicated industrial zones for recycling solar panels and EV batteries

Thailand's Industry Ministry wants to group solar-panel and EV-battery recyclers with hazardous-waste facilities in controlled zones with shared wastewater treatment and round-the-clock monitoring, warning that panels installed about a decade ago could become significant waste within one to two years. It is also reviewing decades-old laws that cap dumping fines at 200,000 baht, with fines of up to tens of millions of baht and asset seizures under discussion.

Why it matters: India faces the same wave of first-generation solar panels and has no separate solar-waste rulebook yet, so Thailand's zone-plus-penalty model is a template Indian recyclers and regulators may be asked to match.

Source: Bangkok Post (via Now.Solar)

Rare earthsWorld16 Oct 2026

Ex-China rare earth prices hold steady as Lynas agrees A$968 million Meteoric deal

SMM's ex-China weekly review put Pr-Nd oxide at $115-129/kg FOB and terbium oxide at $1,161-1,249/kg FOB, down $20/kg, with most other prices flat and trading thin. Lynas agreed to buy Meteoric Resources for about A$968 million to add Brazil's Caldeira ionic clay project, while Arafura raised its binding NdPr oxide offtake to 850 tonnes a year.

Why it matters: Ex-China oxide prices set the value of recovered Nd, Pr and Tb in magnet scrap, so Indian magnet recyclers and the new magnet-scheme bidders should track these benchmarks rather than Chinese domestic prices.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Black massWorld16 Oct 2026

Jereh plans 85,000-tonne lithium battery recycling project in Jinan, China

Jereh Resources Recycling Technology has opened environmental-impact review for a plant in Jinan, Shandong, with a 25,000 t/yr line for scrap LFP batteries and a 60,000 t/yr line for LFP and ternary batteries. The process runs from pack dismantling and crushing to oxygen-free pyrolysis and sorting, with black mass, copper and aluminium as outputs.

Why it matters: More Chinese pre-treatment capacity means more competition for global battery scrap and black mass, which can weigh on the prices Indian sellers are offered.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

PolicyWorld16 Oct 2026

Malaysia discloses battery-waste volumes for the first time, but has no EV battery EPR yet

A written parliamentary reply from Malaysia's environment ministry said 13,743 tonnes of SW103 battery waste were generated from 2021 to 2025, with seven licensed off-site recycling facilities operating as of March 2026. A mandatory EPR regime for EV battery recycling is still under study, and the Department of Environment is drafting amendments to blacklist repeat offenders.

Why it matters: India already runs battery EPR targets; neighbours without them remain loosely regulated sources of scrap, and a policy gap to watch for Indian recyclers sourcing in the region.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

Battery recyclingWorld15 Oct 2026

EU names cylib's Dormagen battery recycling plant a Strategic Project

The European Commission picked German recycler cylib's project as a Strategic Project under the Critical Raw Materials Act; its hydrometallurgical plant in Dormagen, now under construction, is designed to handle up to 140,000 EV batteries a year. The label backs the Act's aim of meeting at least 25% of EU strategic raw material demand from recycling by 2030.

Why it matters: Europe is lining up permitting and finance support for domestic battery recycling, which will shape where black mass and recycled metals flow and the standard Indian recyclers selling into EU supply chains will be compared against.

Source: chemeurope.com

Black massWorld15 Oct 2026

China black mass payables slip after the National Day holiday as lithium and cobalt fall

SMM put ternary electrode black mass payables at about 71–73% on 15 October, around 1.2 points below the first day after the holiday, while LFP electrode black mass fell 975 yuan per lithium percentage point to 4,800–5,300 yuan. Lithium carbonate was quoted at 101,800–107,000 yuan a tonne and cobalt sulphate is down more than 4,000 yuan a tonne since the break.

Why it matters: Chinese payables are a key reference for Indian black mass deals, so softer metal prices point to thinner margins for recyclers selling or buying this quarter.

Source: SMM (Shanghai Metals Market)

Battery recyclingWorld15 Oct 2026

Redwood Materials will now recycle Rivian's battery production scrap

Rivian has widened its partnership with Redwood Materials, which will recycle scrap from Rivian's battery manufacturing on top of handling end-of-life packs. Redwood is also building a 10 MWh storage system at Rivian's Normal, Illinois plant from more than 100 second-life Rivian packs.

Why it matters: Production scrap is the cleanest, highest-value feedstock in battery recycling, and Indian recyclers chasing cell-maker scrap will face the same OEM-tied deals as gigafactories ramp up.

Source: EV Infrastructure News

Rare earthsWorld15 Oct 2026

China's NdFeB scrap prices hold near highs as recyclers hesitate

SMM's weekly review put recycled Pr-Nd at 755–765 yuan/kg, dysprosium at 1,230–1,250 yuan/kg and terbium at 5,600–5,800 yuan/kg, with Pr-Nd edging up mid-week alongside oxide prices. Recyclers were cautious about paying more, and SMM expects Pr-Nd scrap to consolidate at high levels.

Why it matters: Chinese scrap prices set the benchmark for what magnet swarf and end-of-life motors from India can fetch.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld14 Oct 2026

Ionic Rare Earths files plans for a commercial magnet recycling plant in Belfast

Ionic Rare Earths submitted a planning application to Belfast City Council on 19 September for a commercial magnet recycling facility at Queen's Island. A decision is expected in Q1 2027, construction is planned from Q3 2027, and the company is working to secure a £12 million UK government capital grant.

Why it matters: Magnet-to-oxide recycling outside China is moving towards commercial scale, which is useful as a benchmark for Indian firms eyeing rare earth recovery from e-waste and motor scrap.

Source: Mining Technology

Black massWorld14 Oct 2026

Fastmarkets restarts US black mass payables after export exemptions free up flows

Fastmarkets resumed its ex-works US nickel and cobalt black mass payable assessments on 14 October. It had suspended them on 1 September, after a US Commerce Department rule banning black mass exports took effect on 27 August.

Why it matters: With the US price reference back, Indian buyers and refiners can again track US black mass against Asian payables. It also shows US export exemptions are letting some material move.

Source: Fastmarkets

E-wasteWorld14 Oct 2026

EU sold 14.6 million tonnes of electronics in 2024 but collected only 5.5 million tonnes of e-waste

Citing Eurostat data for 2024, the European Environmental Bureau and other NGOs said electronics sales in the EU have risen 91% since 2012, while e-waste collection grew about 6% year on year to 5.5 million tonnes. Germany's collection rate was 28.7%, and preparing-for-reuse rates stay below 2%, as the groups push for tougher rules in the WEEE Directive revision.

Why it matters: A tighter EU WEEE regime will shape the compliance costs of global brands that also sell in India, and Europe's weak collection numbers are a reminder that EPR targets only work when collection is enforced.

Source: European Environmental Bureau

E-wasteWorld14 Oct 2026

E-Waste Day 2026 targets cheap 'fast tech' like cables, earbuds and vapes

A WEEE Forum study found that only 32% of low-cost gadgets were sent for recycling or reuse, while 39% of buyers had finished with them within a month. The UN estimates 62 million tonnes of e-scrap are generated each year, with only 22.3% recycled.

Why it matters: Small, low-value items are the hardest category to collect against EPR targets, and their embedded cells add to fire risk at recycling plants.

Source: Resource Recycling

Battery recyclingWorld14 Oct 2026

Singapore's NEU Battery Materials raises $7.6M and opens an LFP lithium recovery plant

NEU closed a $7.6 million round co-led by Shift4Good and SGInnovate. It opened a plant in Tuas that can process about 1,300 tonnes a year of LFP scrap and black mass into battery-grade lithium. Europe and US expansion is planned from 2027.

Why it matters: LFP is the hard case for recyclers because it holds no nickel or cobalt. Lithium-only recovery economics will matter as India's LFP volumes grow.

Source: TechNode Global

EPRWorld13 Oct 2026

Sri Lanka moves towards an EPR-based national e-waste system

Sri Lanka's Environment Ministry is preparing amendments to the National Environmental Act and seeking approval for a national e-waste policy built around extended producer responsibility. Specific producer obligations have not yet been announced.

Why it matters: Another South Asian market adopting producer responsibility could open compliance and recycling work for Indian EPR service providers and recyclers next door.

Source: Business News (Sri Lanka)

Solar wasteWorld13 Oct 2026

New South Wales consults on Australia's first mandatory solar panel recycling scheme

The NSW government has released a draft regulation and issues paper that would make solar panel brand owners pay for collecting and recycling end-of-life panels. The state has more than 1.18 million rooftop systems and about 14,000 tonnes of panel waste a year, expected to reach 89,000 tonnes by 2045.

Why it matters: It is a live example of solar-specific producer responsibility, which is the model Indian stakeholders are now asking for.

Source: NSW Government (via Solar Now)

Rare earthsWorld6 Oct 2026

Ex-China terbium and dysprosium prices jump on US demand

Fastmarkets' CIP global terbium oxide assessment rose to $5,000–8,000/kg, up 41% in a week. Dysprosium oxide moved to $2,500–3,500/kg. China's export controls keep these heavy rare earths scarce outside China.

Why it matters: Outside China, these magnet metals now sell at many times China's domestic price. That premium is what makes magnet recycling pay.

Source: Fastmarkets

Battery recyclingWorld5 Oct 2026

GM puts cathode made from recycled EV batteries into production vehicles

With Cirba Solutions, GM turned 80 end-of-life EV packs into black mass and then more than 12 tonnes of new cathode material for Ultium Cells. The first cars built with it have come off the line in Michigan and Tennessee.

Why it matters: This is closed-loop proof at automaker scale. It is the benchmark Indian OEMs and recyclers will be measured against.

Source: Resource Recycling

Black massWorld2 Oct 2026

US lets ABTC export up to $100M of black mass despite export controls

The US Commerce Department granted American Battery Technology Co. an exception to sell up to $100 million of recycled black mass abroad. US rules otherwise require black mass producers to sell their output domestically until August 2027.

Why it matters: US shredding capacity has run ahead of refining. Exceptions like this decide where black mass flows, and that affects payables in Asia.

Source: Waste Dive
