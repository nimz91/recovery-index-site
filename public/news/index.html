E-waste, batteries and recycling news

The stories that move India's recovery markets: EPR rules, e-waste, black mass, battery and rare earth recycling. India first, then the world. Summaries are ours; every story links to its source.

Battery recyclingWorld4 Nov 2026

Wagga residents press NSW planning commission to reject a 3,000-tonne lithium-ion battery recycling hub

The NSW Independent Planning Commission held public meetings on 3 and 4 November on Calibre Metals' proposed battery recycling hub in East Wagga, which the developer says could process at most 3,000 tonnes a year. The planning department received more than 50 community submissions opposing it, with flood risk a recurring concern, and written submissions stay open until 11 November.

Why it matters: Local opposition over fire and flood risk is a hurdle Indian battery recyclers also face when siting plants, and shows why site choice and safety cases matter for approvals.

Source: The Daily Advertiser

Critical mineralsIndia4 Nov 2026

Cabinet clears ₹2,814 crore capex subsidy for two lithium and two nickel processing plants

The five-year scheme (FY27 to FY31) offers a 30% capital subsidy to four units, each lithium plant needing at least 30,000 tonnes of capacity and each nickel plant at least 50,000 tonnes, with cobalt recovered as a by-product of nickel. Support is capped at ₹500 crore per lithium beneficiary and ₹900 crore per nickel beneficiary, picked through technical qualification followed by bidding on the lowest capital support sought.

Why it matters: It funds the step of turning ores and intermediates into high-purity battery compounds, which India largely lacks today; whether recycled intermediates such as black mass qualify will decide how much it helps Indian battery recyclers.

Source: Business Standard

EPRIndia3 Nov 2026

Lead industry body wants India's battery dealers made accountable for collecting spent batteries

India Lead Zinc Development Association executive director L Pugazhenthy says the Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 leave dealers' duties unclear, and wants dealers registered, collections reported and recyclers audited regularly rather than approved once. About 30% of India's recycled lead still comes from the informal sector and about 65% from authorised recyclers, even though authorised capacity of about 3.5 million tonnes is well above secondary lead output of about 1.6 million tonnes.

Why it matters: Tighter dealer and audit rules would steer more spent lead-acid batteries to authorised recyclers and change how producers meet their battery EPR targets.

Source: Batteries International

DealsWorld3 Nov 2026

Malaysian battery recycler Econili adds Vynn Capital, taking total funding to RM8 million

Econili, one of five licensed battery-recycling operators in Malaysia, has 24,000 tonnes a year of battery treatment and 12,000 tonnes of critical-mineral recovery capacity in Ipoh, recovering lithium, nickel and cobalt. The undisclosed Vynn Capital cheque brings total funding to about RM8 million (US$1.96 million), to be used to buy more feedstock and raise throughput.

Why it matters: Southeast Asian recyclers raising money to buy more battery scrap adds to regional competition for the feedstock Indian recyclers also import.

Source: Digital News Asia

E-wasteIndia31 Oct 2026

Wayanad gathers 80 tonnes of e-waste in a 50-day drive to become India's first e-waste-free district

The Kerala campaign, launched on 10 September, covered all 23 village panchayats and three municipalities, with government offices handing in 31 tonnes and Noolpuzha becoming the first e-waste-free panchayat on 27 October after collecting 5,423 kg. A buffer period with a complaints call centre runs to 10 November, before a formal declaration at Kalpetta on 20 November.

Why it matters: If other districts copy this government-led model, more household and office e-waste could move into formal collection, the feedstock India's EPR-registered recyclers compete for.

Source: Onmanorama

PolicyIndia31 Oct 2026

GST reverse charge now applies to e-waste scrap bought from unregistered sellers

From 1 November 2026, a registered business that buys electrical and electronic waste and scrap (tariff item 8549) from an unregistered supplier must pay the GST itself under reverse charge, under Notification No. 04/2026 dated 27 October 2026. The same change covers plastic scrap, tyre scrap and used cooking oil, and was made on the GST Council's recommendation.

Why it matters: Recyclers and dismantlers that source e-waste from informal kabadiwalas now carry the GST liability and paperwork on those purchases, which changes sourcing costs and pushes the chain toward registered suppliers.

Source: SCC Times (SCC Online)

Rare earthsIndia29 Oct 2026

Odisha plans a rare-earth corridor around Gopalpur, from beach-sand mining to magnet making

Odisha is drawing up an integrated rare-earth cluster on the Ganjam-Puri coast covering extraction, separation, metals and permanent magnets, building on its roughly 3.16 million tonnes of monazite (of 13.15 million tonnes nationally). The state has approved five projects with proposed investment of about ₹26,450 crore and around 18,450 jobs, while the Mining Corporation of Odisha is acquiring new beach-sand blocks.

Why it matters: Rare earth chloride from IREL's Odisha plant is now processed elsewhere, so a local magnet supply chain would create domestic offtake, and future scrap streams, for India's planned magnet recyclers.

Source: Business Standard

DealsIndia29 Oct 2026

Gravita's September-quarter revenue rises 38% to ₹1,427 crore as its new copper business adds volume

Gravita India reported Q2 FY27 consolidated revenue of ₹1,427 crore and net profit of ₹102 crore (up 6%), with the integrated copper business contributing ₹420 crore. Lead recycling volume fell 11.3% to 39,203 tonnes on scrap shortages linked to West Asia and Red Sea shipping delays, and the company plans ₹1,680 crore of capex through FY29, including ₹865 crore for new verticals such as copper and lithium-ion battery recycling.

Why it matters: A listed Indian recycler is committing capital to lithium-ion and copper recycling, while its lead numbers show how exposed scrap-fed recyclers are to import supply disruptions.

Source: Business Today

EPRIndia28 Oct 2026

India's official e-waste numbers and TERI's estimate are still far apart, and EPR checks rely largely on paperwork

CPCB figures given to the Lok Sabha show India generated about 1.397 million tonnes of e-waste in 2024-25 and recycled about 1.159 million tonnes, while a TERI report prepared with NITI Aayog support estimates 6.19 million tonnes for 2024, with only 22% reaching authorised recyclers. TERI, which counts 381 registered recyclers and 128 refurbishers, says EPR audits rely mostly on paper checklists and that the GSTN and EPR portals are not linked, weakening invoice tracing.

Why it matters: If regulators move to material-balance tests or third-party audits of recyclers, as industry voices in the piece propose, the supply and price of EPR certificates that producers buy could shift.

Source: BW Businessworld

Critical mineralsIndia27 Oct 2026

MIDHANI signs MoU with BARC and the Heavy Water Board to recover nickel and cobalt from plant reverts

Defence PSU Mishra Dhatu Nigam (MIDHANI) signed a three-way MoU with the Heavy Water Board and BARC to develop efficient recovery processes for nickel and cobalt from its plant reverts. The pact is a cooperation framework; no capacity, investment or timeline was disclosed.

Why it matters: It brings state research labs into recovering nickel and cobalt from secondary material, the same metals India's battery recyclers and the critical-minerals push are targeting.

Source: PSU Watch

EPRIndia22 Oct 2026

Bhupender Yadav says over 86,000 producers and 4,880 recyclers are now registered under India's EPR rules

Speaking at a CII Circular Economy Conference in New Delhi, the Environment Minister said more than 86,043 producers and 4,880 recyclers are registered under EPR frameworks covering plastic, battery, e-waste and tyre waste. He said nearly 499.60 lakh tonnes of waste have been processed across these regulated streams so far.

Why it matters: The minister tied EPR certificate transfers to recycler income and capacity growth, signalling the government sees the certificate market as the main lever for formal recycling.

Source: ThePrint (via PTI)

PolicyIndia20 Oct 2026

Mines Ministry panel to rewrite the 2019 National Mineral Policy, naming recycling and critical minerals as priorities

The Mines Ministry has formed a nine-member panel, chaired by an Additional Secretary, to review the National Mineral Policy, 2019 and recommend a comprehensive revision within two months, PTI reports citing sources. Recycling, critical minerals and deep-sea resources are named as the new priorities, alongside a planned recast of the MMDR Act, 1957.

Why it matters: If recycling gets a formal place in national mineral policy, it could shape future incentives and rules for India's e-waste and battery recyclers.

Source: Business Standard (via PTI)

World roundup

Solar wasteWorld2 Nov 2026

Japan proposes that owners scrapping 50 tonnes or more of solar panels file disposal plans in advance

Japan's environment ministry and METI favour a 50-tonne threshold that would cover about 7,398 facilities and roughly 38% of expected panel waste from 2027 to 2050, under a solar recycling law due to take effect around December 2027. Panel waste is now projected to peak at up to about 500,000 tonnes a year in the 2040s, well above earlier estimates.

Why it matters: Japan's volume-based trigger is a working template for India as it decides how to make large solar plant owners handle their own coming wave of end-of-life modules.

Source: pv magazine via Solar Now

Black massWorld2 Nov 2026

Sumitomo moves past an MoU to study black mass processing at Livium's Melbourne site

Sumitomo Corporation and Livium's Envirostream unit are now evaluating technology, equipment, economics and approvals for expanding black mass output at Derrimut, Victoria, with possible refining in Japan. Sumitomo has started onboarding for a potential black mass purchase, but no binding deal, volumes or pricing are agreed, and Envirostream's existing SungEel HiTech offtake runs to June 2027.

Why it matters: Japanese trading houses lining up black mass from smaller markets is the same feedstock competition Indian recyclers face as they try to keep black mass at home.

Source: Mining.com.au

Rare earthsWorld2 Nov 2026

Fraunhofer and TU Freiberg test two new routes to recover neodymium from hard-drive magnets

The four-year, Horizon Europe-funded NEO-CYCLE project is trialling solid-state chlorination with ammonium chloride and an electrochemical process to separate neodymium, iron and boron from scrapped magnets, with a pilot plant already being planned. Fraunhofer notes about 90% of Europe's neodymium comes from China and most magnets collected in Europe are shipped there for reprocessing.

Why it matters: Hard drives are a large part of India's e-waste stream, so low-chemical magnet recovery routes are directly relevant to recyclers eyeing rare earths under the critical-mineral recycling scheme.

Source: Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft

Solar wasteWorld1 Nov 2026

Comstock Metals says its Nevada solar panel recycling line now runs continuously

Comstock Metals reports that its solar panel recycling system at Silver Springs, Nevada has moved to continuous operation across four shift teams and will now ramp up production. It also says extra storage for incoming panels is in place at its Hanford, California and Cambridge, Ohio sites.

Why it matters: Commercial-scale panel recycling running round the clock in the US is a reference point for India, which has yet to build dedicated capacity for its own coming wave of solar waste.

Source: American Recycler via Now.Solar

Battery recyclingWorld30 Oct 2026

China's GEM recycled 50,105 tonnes of EV batteries in January–September, up over 36%

GEM, one of China's largest battery recyclers, says it recycled and dismantled 50,105 tonnes of power batteries in the first three quarters of 2026. That is more than 36% higher than a year earlier, according to its third-quarter report.

Why it matters: Fast-growing Chinese recyclers set the scale and cost benchmark that Indian battery recyclers and black mass buyers will have to compete against.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Solar wasteWorld30 Oct 2026

Australia faces 1.1 million tonnes of dead solar panels by 2035 and needs national rules now, RAND–RMIT brief warns

A policy brief by RAND and RMIT University, sponsored by Australia's industry department, projects cumulative end-of-life solar waste of 1.1 million tonnes by 2035, possibly tripling by 2050, and urges a national PV circularity framework by mid-2027 and federal funding for recycling plants by 2028. It values recoverable silver at more than A$6 billion (about US$4.2 billion) a year by 2035 and finds a 2,500-tonne-a-year recycling plant could pay back in 7.2 to 8 years in five states.

Why it matters: India's solar fleet is growing far faster than Australia's, so the brief's plant economics and reuse-first approach are a useful benchmark for Indian recyclers planning panel capacity.

Source: pv magazine Australia via Now.Solar

PolicyWorld29 Oct 2026

European clean-tech groups ask the EU to weigh export fees on black mass as most of it leaves the bloc

Transport & Environment, Recharge, Clean Tech for Europe and EIES urged the EU to assess export fees or tariffs on black mass and untreated battery waste in its Circular Economy Act, along with a single customs code and end-of-waste criteria for the material. T&E estimates at least 60% of Europe's black mass is exported for refining and says over half of the region's battery recycling capacity is at risk.

Why it matters: Curbs on European black mass exports would shrink the traded supply that refiners elsewhere, including India's new hydrometallurgy plants, compete for.

Source: Transport & Environment via CleanTechnica

Black massWorld29 Oct 2026

China black mass payables slip again as lithium carbonate falls back to about 90,000 yuan a tonne

Lithium carbonate futures jumped early in the week but were back near 90,000 yuan a tonne by 29 October, SMM reports, and LFP black mass prices held at first before easing with them. Ternary and LCO payable indicators edged down alongside nickel, cobalt and lithium and now sit near their lowest in about a year, with cobalt payables settling below nickel and lithium.

Why it matters: Chinese payables are the benchmark for black mass sold out of India, so weaker payables cut what Indian shredders can earn on their output.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld29 Oct 2026

China's recycled Pr-Nd from magnet scrap slips to 715–720 yuan/kg as oxide prices weaken

SMM says recycled Pr-Nd from NdFeB scrap fell through the week to 715–720 yuan/kg as recyclers cut their quotes in step with lower Pr-Nd oxide, while dysprosium (1,230–1,250 yuan/kg) and terbium (5,600–5,800 yuan/kg) held steady. Suppliers resisted selling at lower prices, trading stayed thin, and SMM expects scrap prices to remain weak in the short term.

Why it matters: Scrap Pr-Nd prices shape the economics of the magnet-recycling capacity Indian firms are planning alongside the rare earth magnet scheme.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Solar wasteWorld28 Oct 2026

Australia's A$24.7 million solar panel recycling pilot has been on hold for over five months

The federal pilot, meant to set up as many as 100 collection sites for old panels, was suspended on 15 May after a single complaint under procurement rules, and the environment department could not say when it will restart. Australia had about 3 million panels (60,000 tonnes) reach end of life in 2025, a figure that could hit 50 million panels (1 million tonnes) by 2035, with as little as 17% recycled.

Why it matters: India's own solar fleet will throw up similar volumes, and Australia shows how slowly collection systems get built without a clear, enforced rulebook.

Source: AAP via Solar Now

Black massWorld27 Oct 2026

Korean black mass payables drop to their lowest since January as LME Week mood sours

Fastmarkets' NCM black mass payables (cif South Korea) fell to 88-94% for nickel and cobalt on 21 October, from 92-96% a week earlier, the weakest it has recorded since 21 January 2026. Traders at LME Week pointed to Korean cellmakers moving to LFP, with one recycler saying Korean pCAM and CAM plants are running at only about 20% capacity.

Why it matters: Lower Asian payables cut what Indian recyclers can earn by exporting NCM black mass and strengthen the case for refining it at home.

Source: Fastmarkets

Rare earthsWorld27 Oct 2026

HyProMag USA plans three more US magnet recycling plants to quadruple output to about 4,200 tonnes a year

A pre-feasibility study puts new plants in South Carolina, Nevada and a second Texas site at about $623–656 million, lifting salable NdFeB magnet output to roughly 4,192 tonnes a year by 2034 from about 1,048 tonnes at its first plant in Northlake, Texas. The study, which modelled a recycled-scrap-only case among others, estimates a post-tax NPV of $1.85–2.06 billion; Northlake's first phase is due in mid-2028 and no offtake deals are signed yet.

Why it matters: Magnet scrap is becoming a contested feedstock outside China, which matters for Indian recyclers that collect NdFeB scrap from motors and electronics and for India's planned magnet plants.

Source: Investing.com

PolicyWorld26 Oct 2026

African governments move towards EPR for solar panels and batteries as installations surge

Nigeria has validated a policy that would make producers, distributors and importers of off-grid solar gear responsible for it at end of life, pending cabinet approval, and ECOWAS energy ministers adopted a regional framework on handling and recycling storage batteries. Ember expects Africa to install a record 17 GW of solar in 2026, roughly 100,000 panels a day.

Why it matters: New EPR regimes in fast-growing solar markets could open export and partnership routes for Indian panel and battery recyclers.

Source: Climate Home News

Rare earthsWorld24 Oct 2026

Taiwan's ITRI plans a 500-tonne-a-year rare earth plant to meet half the island's needs by 2030

ITRI president Chang Pei-zen says a demonstration plant making 500 tonnes of rare earths a year is targeted for completion by end-2028, a hundredfold scale-up from the institute's experimental line of about 5,000 kg a year. The goal is to cover half of Taiwan's demand by end-2030 and cut exposure to Chinese export restrictions, with state funding and possible price guarantees still under discussion.

Why it matters: It is another electronics-heavy economy building rare earth separation outside China, the same supply gap India's magnet and critical-mineral recycling schemes are trying to close.

Source: Focus Taiwan (CNA)

Solar wasteWorld24 Oct 2026

South Korea starts recycling solar panels on site with mobile trailer plants as panel waste climbs

Waste solar panels processed in South Korea rose from 152 tonnes in 2023 to 2,300 tonnes in 2026, and the first on-site recycling with trailer-mounted units ran this week under a regulatory sandbox that exempts it from the Wastes Control Act. A recycler says processing at the solar plant can cut transport costs to about one-sixth, while a blade-based method that separates glass from cells lifts silver and copper recovery four- to fivefold.

Why it matters: As India's early solar fleet ages, on-site processing and better silver recovery show Indian recyclers ways to cut the freight costs that make panel recycling hard to pay for.

Source: SBS News

Solar wasteWorld23 Oct 2026

Rystad puts the value of recyclable solar panel materials at €1.7 billion today, rising to €69 billion by 2050

Rystad Energy estimates recycled silver could cover 21% of solar manufacturers' needs in 2035, against only about 2% for copper. Separately, a University of Newcastle pilot processed material equivalent to 468 kg of rooftop panels and recovered close to 100% of the silver.

Why it matters: Silver is the main value driver in end-of-life panels, so Indian recyclers preparing for solar waste under the e-waste EPR regime need recovery routes that capture it.

Source: Recycling International via Now.Solar

PolicyWorld22 Oct 2026

Thailand proposes banning metal scrap imports to curb e-waste smuggling

Thailand's Customs Department on 21 October proposed a ban on imports of ferrous, copper, aluminium and rubber scrap, with a 6–12 month transition period, to fight smuggling of electronic and hazardous waste. Customs has already stepped up physical checks on containers declared as metal scrap, though the ban itself is still only a proposal.

Why it matters: A Thai ban would push more scrap toward other Asian importers such as India and signals tighter regional policing of e-waste shipped as metal scrap.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

GM ends magnet supply deal with VAC's new $500 million South Carolina plant

General Motors terminated its strategic sourcing agreement with e-VAC Magnetics, the US unit of Germany's Vacuumschmelze, effective 1 July 2026; the plant has capacity of just under 2,000 tonnes a year and was meant to send 90% of its output to GM. VAC has begun arbitration seeking damages, and Energy Fuels, which agreed in June to buy VAC for $1.9 billion, says the price could be adjusted depending on the outcome.

Why it matters: Weaker carmaker demand for non-Chinese magnets shapes the market Indian magnet makers and rare-earth recyclers are planning to sell into.

Source: Reuters via StreetInsider

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

Neo Performance Materials wins up to €126 million in EU funding to expand its Estonian magnet plant

The EU Innovation Fund grant will help lift Neo's magnet capacity in Estonia from 2,000 to about 5,000 tonnes a year, with a third of the new output aimed at EU offshore wind. The grant agreement is due to be signed in March 2027, and construction is expected to take two to three years.

Why it matters: Europe is paying to build magnet capacity outside China, a benchmark for the incentives India offers its own rare-earth magnet and recycling projects.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Black massWorld22 Oct 2026

China black mass payables slide again as lithium carbonate falls about 3,000 yuan in a week

SMM's weekly recycling review says lithium carbonate started the week near 100,000 yuan/mt and was quoted about 3,000 yuan lower by 22 October, dragging recycled material prices down. Ternary black mass payables kept grinding lower, while LCO black mass payables fell faster on weak demand.

Why it matters: Chinese payables set the reference for Asian black mass trade, so falling coefficients squeeze margins for Indian recyclers selling or importing black mass.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld22 Oct 2026

China's NdFeB scrap Pr-Nd prices keep sliding as oxide prices weaken

Shanghai Metals Market says praseodymium-neodymium recovered from NdFeB magnet scrap fell again this week to 735-745 yuan/kg, tracking lower Pr-Nd oxide prices, while scrap dysprosium closed at 1,230-1,250 yuan/kg and terbium at 5,600-5,800 yuan/kg. Recyclers cut their quotes and bought less, holders were reluctant to sell, and SMM expects prices to stay weak in the short term.

Why it matters: Chinese scrap magnet prices set the benchmark for what Indian e-waste and motor recyclers can expect for recovered magnets, so a falling market squeezes margins on magnet recovery projects.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld20 Oct 2026

China's rare earth magnet exports hit a 2026 high of about 6,165 tonnes in September

China exported 6,165 tonnes of rare earth permanent magnets in September, up 23% from August and 6.7% from a year earlier, the highest monthly figure this year, as backlogged orders were released, according to SMM. Exports for January to September totalled 48,055 tonnes, up 20% year on year.

Why it matters: SMM counts India among the markets driving demand, and steadier Chinese magnet supply sets the price bar that India's planned magnet plants and magnet recyclers must compete with.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Rare earthsWorld20 Oct 2026

Phoenix Tailings plans $375 million rare earth separation plant in New York that will also take recycled feed

Phoenix Tailings will build a plant in Rochester, New York, to process more than 18,000 tons a year of mined and recycled rare earth feedstock into over 4,400 tons of separated oxides, ramping up in 2027 and reaching full capacity in 2028. It is part of a $1.2 billion project backed by a $500 million conditional loan commitment from the US Office of Strategic Capital.

Why it matters: Large Western separation capacity that accepts recycled feed creates more buyers for magnet scrap outside China, a market Indian magnet recyclers could target.

Source: MINING.COM

Critical mineralsWorld20 Oct 2026

Öko-Institut study says recycling batteries in the EU could cut virgin cobalt demand by 60-70%

An Öko-Institut study commissioned by members of the EU Raw Materials Coalition finds that recycling lithium-ion batteries inside the EU could reduce virgin demand by 25-50% for lithium, 60-70% for cobalt and 35-50% for nickel. It also says smaller EVs and lighter batteries could trim mineral needs by about 17% by 2035, and calls for specific recycling targets and more funding for recycling plants.

Why it matters: It gives Indian policymakers and recyclers a benchmark for how much recycling can offset imported lithium, cobalt and nickel as India builds out its own critical-mineral recycling incentives.

Source: Resource Recycling

Battery recyclingWorld20 Oct 2026

Korea's recyclers struggle to make money from LFP batteries as their share of EVs grows

LFP cells held over 65% of the global EV cathode market in January–August 2026, but their recoverable value rests mostly on lithium (about $9.59/kg last year) rather than cobalt ($34.21/kg) or nickel ($15.2/kg). Korea recovered 65.9% of mandatory-return EV batteries between 2021 and August 2026, and industry is asking for recycled-content mandates and clarity on who pays to treat low-value LFP packs.

Why it matters: India's EV and storage fleet is leaning heavily on LFP, so Indian recyclers and EPR planners face the same question of how to fund recycling of chemistries with little cobalt or nickel to sell.

Source: MoneyToday (Korea)

Black massWorld19 Oct 2026

China's black mass output fell about 15% in September as LFP supply tightened

SMM estimates China produced roughly 56,000 tonnes of lithium battery black mass in September, about 15% less than in August, with LFP black mass driving most of the drop. Stricter rules on reusing old batteries, plant maintenance and sellers holding back scrap all cut volumes, while falling lithium carbonate and cobalt sulfate prices squeezed recycler margins.

Why it matters: Lower Chinese black mass output and thinner margins affect the payables and export demand Indian battery recyclers see for their own black mass.

Source: Shanghai Metals Market (SMM)

Battery recyclingWorld19 Oct 2026

POSCO's Korean recycling plant turns 12,000 tonnes of black mass a year into battery salts

POSCO's battery recycling unit says its South Korean hydrometallurgy plant processes about 12,000 tonnes of black mass a year into 2,500 tonnes of lithium carbonate, 12,000 tonnes of nickel sulfate, 3,800 tonnes of cobalt sulfate and 2,000 tonnes of manganese sulfate. The plant reports recovering over 90% of the main valuable metals, and SNE Research expects used-battery volumes to grow from 44 GWh in 2025 to 3,339 GWh in 2040.

Why it matters: It is a benchmark for the scale and recovery rates Indian black mass refiners will need to compete with as they move beyond shredding.

Source: MoneyToday

Solar wasteWorld18 Oct 2026

Thailand plans dedicated industrial zones for recycling solar panels and EV batteries

Thailand's Industry Ministry wants to group solar-panel and EV-battery recyclers with hazardous-waste facilities in controlled zones with shared wastewater treatment and round-the-clock monitoring, warning that panels installed about a decade ago could become significant waste within one to two years. It is also reviewing decades-old laws that cap dumping fines at 200,000 baht, with fines of up to tens of millions of baht and asset seizures under discussion.

Why it matters: India faces the same wave of first-generation solar panels and has no separate solar-waste rulebook yet, so Thailand's zone-plus-penalty model is a template Indian recyclers and regulators may be asked to match.

Source: Bangkok Post (via Now.Solar)
